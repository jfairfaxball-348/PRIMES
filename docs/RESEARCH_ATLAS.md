# Initial Research Atlas

These are **search zones**, not novelty claims. Many components are certainly connected to existing literature. Their purpose is to generate experiments whose exact outputs can later be collision-audited.

## Zone A — Cross-scale gap motifs

Represent consecutive prime gaps as finite words and study which motif properties persist when moving from one magnitude range to another.

Questions:

- Which normalized motif rankings are unusually stable?
- Are there motifs whose before/after context is asymmetric?
- Do rare large gaps have reproducible local "shapes" beyond their size?
- Are higher finite-difference motifs more stable than raw-gap motifs?

## Zone B — Residue transition fingerprints

For declared moduli, treat consecutive prime residues as a directed transition word rather than only a marginal distribution.

Questions:

- Which transitions are absent for elementary reasons?
- After removing forced constraints, what conditional structure remains?
- How do transition fingerprints change with modulus and scale?
- Are there exact relationships between fingerprints for related moduli?

## Zone C — Local density event geometry

Identify unusually prime-dense or prime-sparse windows under a predeclared normalization and study the finite configuration around those events.

Questions:

- Are approach and recovery patterns symmetric?
- Do extremal windows have recurring canonical shapes?
- Can local event structure be represented by a small invariant?
- Does the same invariant describe both dense and sparse extremes?

## Zone D — Index/value dual transforms

Construct exact transforms that exchange or compose prime index and prime value information.

Questions:

- Which local invariants survive the exchange?
- Can apparently different subsequences be unified by one operator?
- Are there finite forbidden patterns under iteration?

High collision risk: prior literature is expected and audits must be aggressive.

## Zone E — Higher-difference symbolic dynamics

Study finite differences of `p_n` beyond the first gap, encoded as exact sign/magnitude motifs.

Questions:

- Are there persistent forbidden sign words after trivial constraints are removed?
- Do record events in one difference order predict structure in another?
- Are there exact implications between short motifs?

## Zone F — Prime neighbourhoods as finite objects

For each prime, encode a fixed-radius neighbourhood as a canonical finite object: vector, labelled graph, occupancy word, or metric pattern.

Questions:

- Which invariants cluster unexpectedly?
- Are some local object types forbidden?
- Can one define natural partial orders or transformations between neighbourhood types?
- Do extremal objects recur across scale?

## Zone G — Residual event alignment

For multiple simple counting/density baselines, record extrema and sign-change events of their residuals.

Questions:

- Which events are representation-stable?
- Can event coincidence be stated exactly?
- Is there a simpler hidden variable governing several residuals?

## Zone H — Symbolic complexity as a detector

Apply exact finite-word complexity measures and reproducible compression diagnostics to gap/residue/configuration words.

Use them only to locate low-complexity regions or motifs.

Any promoted result must be translated back into an exact mathematical statement.
